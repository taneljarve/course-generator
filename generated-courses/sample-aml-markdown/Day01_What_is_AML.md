# Day 1: What is AML?

## Learning Objectives

- Understand what anti-money laundering means in practice
- Recognize why regulated companies care about AML controls
- Learn the three classic stages of money laundering

## Key Concepts

- AML
- Financial crime
- Placement, layering, integration
- Risk-based controls

## Guided Explanation

Anti-money laundering, usually shortened to AML, refers to the controls used to detect and prevent money from illegal sources moving through the financial system.

Most AML work is not about memorizing laws word for word. It is about recognizing suspicious patterns, understanding risk, and knowing when to escalate a case for further review.

The classic laundering model is often explained in three stages:

1. Placement: getting illegal funds into the system
2. Layering: making the money trail harder to follow
3. Integration: making the funds appear legitimate
